Antimicrobial Stewardship Ward Round

Aim — To select safe and effective antimicrobial therapy for a septic patient using the local antibiogram, then follow the patient across five days — de-escalating on culture results, switching to oral therapy and stopping at the right time.

Your patient

You are the antimicrobial stewardship pharmacist. A 74-year-old woman is admitted from a nursing home with suspected urosepsis. You will follow her for five days, and the decision you make on each day changes what you face on the next.

  1. Day 1 is a probability judgement: cultures take 48 hours, so empirical therapy must be chosen from the local antibiogram, not from a textbook.
  2. Day 3 the culture and sensitivities return. Broad-spectrum therapy that was correct on day 1 becomes wrong on day 3 if it is not narrowed.
  3. Every day carries a Stewardship Index (spectrum, route and duration) and a Clinical Response that tracks the patient herself.
  4. Under-treating harms this patient. Over-treating harms the next one — and both are scored.

At the end you see her outcome, and the collateral consequence of what you prescribed.
