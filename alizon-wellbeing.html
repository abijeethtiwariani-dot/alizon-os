Wellbeing & Support

Helplines, practical guidance for a screen-heavy programme, and how to tell us when something is wrong.

Studying with Alizon means long stretches in front of a screen — practicals, reports, examinations — often on top of a job or a clinical posting. Wellbeing here is not a slogan about balance. It is the practical question of how you get through a programme without wrecking your sleep, your eyes or your health, and where to turn when something is genuinely wrong.

Working well on a screen-heavy programme

Your eyes

Long portal sessions cause eye strain and headaches. Look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds every 20 minutes, keep the screen slightly below eye level, and blink deliberately — people blink markedly less when reading a screen.

Your back and wrists

Practicals run long. Get the screen to eye height rather than looking down at a laptop, keep your forearms level, and stand up every half hour. Phone-only study is the hardest on your neck; use a larger screen for report writing where you can.

Your sleep

Working through the night before a submission costs more than it gains — recall and judgement both fall sharply on short sleep, and practicals are judged on judgement. Submit what you have and ask about an extension rather than losing the night.

Pacing the work

Each module has practicals, an assignment and a self-assessment. Spreading them across the module is the difference between a programme you can hold alongside a job and one you cannot. Your dashboard shows what is outstanding.

Studying while working

Many Alizon students are already in practice. Sessions are recorded or scheduled by batch wherever possible — check the schedule before assuming you have to choose between a shift and a session.

Ask early

Illness, bereavement, a family emergency, a clinical posting that moves — none of these are unusual and all of them are easier to handle in advance. Raise it through the portal and it reaches the administration directly.
