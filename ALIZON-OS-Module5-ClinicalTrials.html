New to clinical trial phases? Start here

A new medicine climbs a ladder of four phases. Each phase answers one question, and each exposes more people to the drug only after the previous phase showed acceptable risk. To identify a phase, ask in this order:

  1. Is the drug already approved and marketed? If yes → Phase IV (post-marketing surveillance), whatever the size.
  2. Who is being studied? Healthy volunteers → Phase I. Patients with the disease → Phase II or III.
  3. Is it compared against an existing standard treatment or placebo for registration? → Phase III.
  4. Is it finding whether it works and at what dose, in a few hundred patients? → Phase II.

Use the sample size only as a cross-check — never as your first clue. Unsure on a scenario? Open “Need a hint?” in the station.

Clinical trial phases at a glance

PhaseMain objectiveParticipantsTypical number
Phase ISafety, tolerability & pharmacokinetics; dose escalation to find the maximum tolerated doseHealthy volunteers (patients in oncology / cytotoxic studies)20–100
Phase IIEfficacy (proof of concept) and the optimal dose; further safetyPatients with the target disease100–300
Phase IIIComparison with the existing standard treatment or placebo; confirms efficacy for regulatory approvalPatients — large, multicentre, randomised1,000–5,000
Phase IVPost-marketing surveillance — rare and long-term adverse effects in real-world useGeneral population using the marketed drugTens of thousands

Watch the exception: a first-in-human study of a cytotoxic or oncology drug recruits patients rather than healthy volunteers — but it is still Phase I.
