Data Protection, Access Control & Documentation Audit

You hold the data-protection brief at ALIZON SmartCare. Seven access events from last week's audit log are waiting on your desk, flagged by an anomaly engine that ranks them by risk score. You decide which are breaches, which are lawful work that merely looks alarming, and what each one obliges the hospital to do next. The engine's ranking is not the order of seriousness.

Before you start

Each event arrives with a risk score. The audit-log detail stays closed until you open it, and what you opened is recorded. The engine ranks on volume and hour of access — neither of which is the same as lawfulness.

Two indices sit alongside your mark. Patient confidentiality falls when a real breach is cleared. Operational trust falls when lawful clinical work is treated as a breach — an access review that punishes clinicians for doing their jobs will simply be worked around, and then it protects nobody.

Educational simulation on synthetic data. Every user, patient, log line and identifier is invented for teaching. Nothing here describes a real person, a real hospital or a real breach, and nothing this system outputs is legal advice or a regulatory determination.
