New to ethics review? Start here

An Ethics Committee exists for one purpose — to protect the rights, safety and well-being of the participants. Review every protocol in this order:

  1. Is the science sound? A study that cannot answer its question exposes people to risk for nothing — and can never be ethical.
  2. Do the benefits justify the risks? Never approve an unfavourable risk–benefit ratio, or a placebo arm that withholds effective treatment in a serious illness.
  3. Is consent genuinely free and informed? Understandable language, the right to withdraw, and no payment so large it becomes an undue inducement.
  4. Are vulnerable participants protected? Children, pregnant women, prisoners, and economically disadvantaged people need extra safeguards.
  5. Are compensation and insurance in place? Under the New Drugs & Clinical Trials Rules 2019 these are mandatory.

Then separate fixable paperwork (→ Approved with Modifications) from unethical design (→ Rejected). Unsure? Open “Need a hint?” in the committee room.

The three committee decisions

DecisionWhen it appliesExample
✅ ApprovedAll checklist domains adequate; no changes requiredComplete dossier, sound science, valid consent, insurance in place
⚠ Approved with ModificationsDeficiencies that can be corrected on paper without changing the ethics of the studyMissing compensation clause, ICF too technical, unclear data-handling procedure
❌ RejectedA fundamental ethical violation in the design itselfCoercive payment, unfavourable risk–benefit, unsafe placebo arm, unprotected vulnerable group

Committee membership must be multidisciplinary — medical, pharmacy, legal and lay/community members. The Principal Investigator must not vote on their own protocol (conflict of interest).
